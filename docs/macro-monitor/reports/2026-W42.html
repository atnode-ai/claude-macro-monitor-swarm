Weekly macro briefing

Inflation re-accelerating on both sides of the Atlantic while US activity stays firm: a hawkish late-cycle mix, no labour turn

Executive summary

Inflation was the story of the week, and it came from several sources at once. US core CPI rose 0.335% m/m in September, taking the yoy rate to 2.564%, and headline reached 3.738%. PPI ran hotter still: headline 1.343% m/m and core 0.681% m/m, which points to more pass-through into CPI in the coming months. Euro-area HICP rose to 3.75% yoy against a 3.6% expectation, led by energy. Demand did not soften to offset this. Retail sales rose 0.77% m/m and industrial production 0.66%, while initial claims held at 199,000. Financial conditions remain loose but are drifting tighter: NFCI rose for a 2nd week, HY spreads widened 8bp and the 10y-2y curve flattened 18bp. Sentiment is the one soft reading. Taken together, inflation, activity and labour point the same way, which leaves little room for further Fed easing. The open question is whether markets keep pricing that calmly: 5y5y breakevens fell 4bp to 2.31%.

What the data is saying

Inflation and policy

Three inflation sources aligned this week. US headline CPI rose 0.649% m/m to 3.738% yoy, and core rose 0.335% m/m to 2.564% yoy. Core stays below headline, so energy is doing much of the work, but a 0.3% core monthly pace is not consistent with 2% inflation. PPI is the leading edge of the chain and it is hotter: headline 6.338% yoy and core 4.978% yoy. The last logged core PCE reading, for August, was 3.008% yoy, so the Fed's own gauge was already above target before this data. The FOMC cut by 25bp in September to a 3.75% to 4% range. Against hot CPI and PPI, firm spending and low claims, the case for another cut is weak. In Europe, HICP at 3.75% beat the 3.6% expectation by 0.15 points, again energy-led with core around 2.5% per the analyst read (unconfirmed source). That argues for an ECB hold rather than further easing. BoE policy stays at 3.75% with no new data this week.

Labour

Claims show no turn. Initial claims were 199,000, the 4-week average 198,500 and continuing claims 1.71M, flat on the week. These are well below the thresholds that would signal rising layoffs. The softer side of the labour picture is older: September payrolls rose only 29k with unemployment at 4.2%, and August JOLTS openings fell by 256k to 7.079M. So hiring is slow but firing is low. Average hourly earnings rose 3.025% yoy, which keeps wage pressure moderate and suggests the CPI acceleration is not wage-driven. That view rests on one month of payrolls; the next NFP and JOLTS prints are the real test.

Growth, housing and sentiment

Activity data were firm. Retail sales rose 0.77% m/m after 1.29% in August, with ex-auto up 0.72% and yoy growth at 6.29%. Part of this is price, given 3.7% CPI, but the real gain is still positive. Industrial production rose 0.66% m/m and capacity utilization reached 76.85%, so output confirms spending. The control group was not logged, so the read for Q3 GDP consumption is unconfirmed. Q2 GDP was 2.2% SAAR. Housing remains weak: existing sales were 3.99M SAAR, up 1.5% but near cycle lows, and August permits fell 2.1%. With the 10y yield at 5.19%, affordability will keep housing soft. Sentiment is the outlier. UMich prelim October fell 1.8 points to 46.3, near the year's low. Weak sentiment has not shown up in spending yet, so this is a divergence to watch rather than a signal.

Financial conditions

Conditions are loose but moving tighter at the margin. NFCI rose to -0.499, its 2nd weekly rise, and stays well below zero. HY OAS widened 8bp to 3.23% and IG OAS 2bp to 0.84%; these are small moves from tight levels. The curve flattened: 10y-2y fell 18bp to 0.33% and 10y-3m fell 24bp to 0.82%. Front-end yields rose as the market priced fewer cuts, and the 10y fell 8bp, so the flattening is a bull-bear mix that reads as policy repricing rather than growth fear. Breakevens fell 4bp, with 5y5y at 2.31%, so long-run inflation pricing remains anchored despite hot prints. VIX at 16.46 is calm. Nothing here signals stress; widening of 25bp or more in HY alongside a further NFCI rise would.

Regime watch

The alignment this week is inflation plus activity: CPI, PPI and HICP all firmer while retail sales, IP and claims stay strong. That is the opposite of the labour-turn pattern, and it argues for a pause in easing. A regime change toward stagflation would need the demand side to crack while prices stay hot: initial claims above 220,000 or continuing claims above 1.8M, and a negative ex-auto retail print. A soft-landing read would need core CPI back to 0.2% m/m or below and PPI to cool. On the market side, an NFCI move toward zero with HY widening of 25bp or more would mean conditions are starting to restrain growth.
